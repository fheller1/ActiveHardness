Investigating Active Sampling for Hardness Classification with Vision-Based Tactile Sensors

Abstract

Hardness is one of the most important object properties that humans and robots perceive through touch. This paper investigates active sampling strategies for sample-efficient hardness classification with vision-based tactile sensors (VBTS), using information-theoretic measures of model uncertainty. We evaluate three probabilistic classifier models and two active sampling strategies and a random sampling baseline on a hardness classification task, with our own dataset of VBTS samples collected by a robot and a previously published dataset. Additionally, we conducted a human study on the hardness classification task on our dataset. Our findings indicate that the active sampling approaches, driven by variance and entropy as measures of model uncertainty, perform best with accuracy reaching 90%, compared to human accuracy of 48%, demonstrating the effectiveness of using VBTS for hardness classification.
